---
title: Glossary
subhead: The vocabulary the rulebook uses, defined once.
terms:
  - term: Acquisition
    slug: acquisition
    short: Taking an available company deed from the board, for free, into one of your slots.
    long: >-
      Acquisitions happen in phase 4. Following the order of play each player may
      start one available company of the current era, placing a goods counter or
      a ship for it. Starting a company costs nothing, but you must have a free
      slot to hold it, and a production company that has nowhere legal to place
      its first counter cannot be started at all.
  - term: Era
    slug: era
    aliases:
      - eras
    short: One of the game's three ages — a, b and c — each with its own set of company deeds and city cards.
    long: >-
      A new era begins when no companies of the previous era are left to acquire,
      or when only one type remains. New eras bring a fresh set of company deeds
      onto the map and a round of city building. The game ends when era c ends.
  - term: Hull
    slug: hull
    short: How many goods one ship may carry for each production company in a single operations phase.
    long: >-
      Hull size is an R&D track. Each step lets every ship of that player's
      shipping companies be used one more time by each production company. Ships
      are re-used freely by the next production company to operate, so hull size
      limits use per company, not per phase.
  - term: Merger
    slug: merger
    aliases:
      - mergers
    short: Combining two companies of the same type into one, decided by an open auction.
    long: >-
      The announcing player must have researched the Mergers track far enough to
      hold the resulting number of deeds. Bidding opens at the nominal value of
      the two companies and rises in multiples of their combined goods. The
      winner pays the former owners in proportion to the goods each company had
      on the board.
  - term: Nominal value
    slug: nominal-value
    short: The opening bid in a merger — every good on the board for both companies, priced by type.
    long: >-
      Count the goods or ships each merging company has on the board and multiply
      by the value of that good: 10 for ships, 20 rice, 25 spice, 30 rubber,
      35 siap faji, 40 oil. The total is the minimum first bid, even when the
      announcing player already owns both companies.
  - term: Production zone
    slug: production-zone
    short: A block of adjacent areas producing the same good, all belonging to one company.
    long: >-
      Goods move freely within a production zone, so only one area of it needs to
      touch a ship. A company that owns two separate zones — which can only
      happen through a merger — must ship each zone independently.
  - term: Rupiah
    slug: rupiah
    short: The game's currency. Whoever holds the most at the end wins.
    long: >-
      Each player starts with 100 rupiah. Money is earned by selling goods to
      cities and by shipping other players' goods, and spent on bidding in
      mergers, on turn order and on paid expansion. Money moved to the Bank box
      during the turn order bid can never be spent again but still counts for
      victory.
  - term: Siap faji
    slug: siap-faji
    short: Microwave meals — the good made by merging a rice company with a spice company.
    long: >-
      The only merger between companies producing different goods. It cannot be
      formed in era a. The new owner gives up half the merged land areas, rounded
      up, taking only from the edges of the estate, and the remaining areas
      switch to siap faji. Once formed, a siap faji company can merge only with
      another siap faji company.
  - term: Slot
    slug: slot
    aliases:
      - slots
    short: A space on your R&D track for one company. A merged company of several deeds still occupies one slot.
    long: >-
      Slots cap how many companies you may hold at any one time. You cannot
      acquire a company without a free slot, and you may only announce a merger
      whose result you could legally hold — so freeing a slot is often the point
      of a merger rather than a side effect.
---
